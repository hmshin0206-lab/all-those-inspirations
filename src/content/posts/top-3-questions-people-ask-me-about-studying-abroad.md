In my university, there is an annual information session for the students who are considering studying and working abroad. What makes this event even special is the fact that it is not hosted by the university but voluntarily by the school alumni. Due to the COVID-19, however, the event this year had to turn into 100% online and I had the privilege of participating as one of the panels in the MBA/MS sector.

Answering the questions asked by the students reminded me of the days I first dreamed of studying in a foreign country as well as the time that the dream came real when I received the admission letter from Johns Hopkins. Since I understand how difficult it is, as it was to me, to make up the mind and devote time to explore the unknown, I decided to write this post hoping to be a help for those who are considering studying abroad. And here are the three questions that were the most frequently asked to me during the talk.

#### Q. What made you decide to study abroad?

   Ignorance is always bold. I did not have much knowledge but studying abroad had been my life long dream; I always imagined myself living in a foreign country, mingling with people from diverse backgrounds, and embracing our cultural differences. Thus, **For me, it was the question of ‘when’, not ‘whether’.** While I was working at LG, there was the time that I was feeling that my learning curve reached its peak and I no longer learn as much as I used to. That was when I seriously considered getting a master’s degree. Since I am not a risk-taker (in fact, I am the opposite of risk-taker) I decided to prepare for the requirements including the tests and SOP while I was still having a stable income although it might set back the whole schedule for the preparation.

People have asked me if I were ever afraid of getting a new job after graduation. My answer to that question is “Of course”. Going back to an academic field in the middle of your professional career takes a lot of courage, I admit. Though, I believed that there would be a second chance even if I fail this time. **Ignorance is bold but boldness is what makes you act.**

#### Q. Why did you choose the MS course instead of an MBA?

Since my major was business, I did not have the incentive to apply for an MBA of which I thought the curriculum was pretty much the same as what I have already learned during my university. Since I was determined that I wanted to pursue my career as a marketer, I thought having a marketing degree makes me stand out as a specialist in this business world where there are so many MBA graduates.

#### Q. What are the gains and losses of studying abroad?

   The most significant change that a year in the U.S. had on me was that I became a more proactive person. In other words, **I used to think that opportunities would eventually come when I am ready, but living in a foreign country was like surviving in a jungle, making me a “go-getter”.** 

For instance, even though I still find it hard, I got better at talking to people whom I have never met and at building up networks. In addition, I love learning new things and absorbing constant inspirations but I have never thought about sharing them with others because I thought I was not good enough. Getting out of my comfort zone and understanding the differences caused by our cultural backgrounds, I was finally able to get rid of my so-called “perfectionism” which I think disciplined me but restricted me at the same time. This blog was also created while I was in the U.S. to gather and write down my inspirations, which hopefully will be used as my journal as well as my portfolio.

   Adding to that, going back to a full-time student after 5 years of working opened a whole new world and enlightened me that studying what you love is so much fun. Though struggled a lot with English which I am not native at, I enjoyed studying, debating, and presenting in front of others, which resulted in me getting into the [Beta Gamma Sigma](https://www.betagammasigma.org/home), a business honor society for top 10% students.

For me, the only demerit of studying abroad was money. Even though I received a merit scholarship that covered two-thirds of my tuition, it still cost me a lot of money including living costs. Thus, my advice to those who are considering studying abroad is that **you should have a clear vision and a career goal after graduation.** If your purpose is just to learn something new, studying abroad might not be the best option in terms of ROI since there are so many platforms that you can acquire knowledge such as LinkedIn Learning, Coursera, etc. Since it is a lot of money, I advise you to consider the opportunity cost and calculate (or at least estimate) the ROI. Otherwise, it will be a sunk cost, not an investment.
