It has been a long since the last time I posted on this blog. I guess for everyone, 2020 is the year that can never be forgotten even when recalling it after many years are passed. The virus became a pandemic early last year and the economy got paralyzed right after. Some industries such as travel and airline were hurt so badly while some other sectors like tech, e-commerce, and digital gadgets have never been flourished like this before, which were also directly reflected on the stock market. There is a book called ‘*[Factfulness](https://www.amazon.com/Factfulness-audiobook/dp/B07BFDCWZP/ref=sr_1_1?dchild=1&keywords=factfulness&qid=1619615150&s=audible&sr=1-1)*‘ written by Hans Rosling. In this book, he fears the possibility of an unknown virus that is strong enough to engulf the entire world. Given that the book was published in 2018, I was marveled at his insight and acumen.

I had a special year in 2020 as well, due to the pandemic, but also with personal matters; I became a mom and took a year off of work for maternity leave. This is the first time that I was away from work in almost 10 years. Before my maternity leave started, I was thrilled to have a break from work and get some time for myself. I even made a long list of what I wanted to do during the leave, which included mastering Japanese, taking a UI/UX course, practicing yoga, and hanging out with some old friends. It did not take long till I figured out that maternity leave can never be regarded as a “break”. In fact, it was one of the most exhausting and busiest days of my life. Since everything in regards to the baby was something that I have never done or experienced before, I was struggling in every step, sometimes feeling helpless. Making matters worse, COVID gave me no choice but to isolate myself with the baby all day and night.

My long list of plans had to be adjusted accordingly too. Given the circumstance that my time was now 100% dependent on the baby, I could neither “plan” things ahead nor “act” on these plans. So I pivoted my original plan and named it “passive learning”, which encompasses all sorts of learning mostly by reading since it was the one and only way that I could do while holding the baby or sleeping the baby. My reading had already been a daily routine since long ago, but this time, I wanted something more; I wanted not just to read books but to get something out of them. And that is how I ended up being in two different book clubs, one of which I joined and the other one of which I created myself.

## Dormitory Book Club

This is the one I created. After talking with one of my friends from college who was also taking maternity leave, I found that she had had similar needs, reading more books and digesting them properly, and we both agreed that it should not be some kind of a challenge that you finish and move on to the next one as fast as possible. Thanks to (?) COVID, we all have got some time staying at home, so I invited some of my other friends with whom I have spent my college years in the same dormitory. That is how I created this and named it “Dormitory Book Club” (very intuitive, right?).

We made some ground rules: We meet once a month through Zoom. Everyone has a chance to be a host. The host selects the book of the month and prepares some discussion topics. Then we all read the book and write reviews before the meeting, which I believe is a great way to guarantee that we all have read it and to have a chance to express what we have learned through it using our own words. Starting from last Sep, we have read 9 books so far, and are reading our 10th book this month.

## Two Chapter Book Club

This book club, as the name implies, is a group that everyone reads two chapters of a book each day. The club was created by a Korean statistician living in SF and also a YouTuber with over 100,000 subscribers (Click [here](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC256C9U-lYhr5-Or7vvCrvQ) to check out her amazing channel). I have been subscribing to her channel for a long time and her stories of studying abroad, struggling with her English resonated with me a lot. Then I saw her notice regarding this book club and e-mailed her immediately. The main reason that I took part in this club was that I wanted to read more English books. Books that are written in foreign languages usually require a lot more patience and consistency than the books in your mother tongue, so I wanted to drop a little bit of spur that makes me read an English book every day.

However, as I was more engaged in this club, another element that fascinated me more than my original purpose of joining this club was the people there. While the “Dormitory Book Club” is a group of an inner circle, the members of the “Two Chapter Book Club” are from various backgrounds, locations, and occupations. The only common thing we have is that we all have a desire to learn constantly and are ready to be inspired every single day. The club only has one simple rule: post the summary of the pages you have read that day. There is no forced power involved or any fine system but all the members are so passionate about summarizing and sharing insights of the books they are reading. When I post my summary of a book, for example, people leave comments about their thoughts. Since the discussions are so productive and inspiring, I feel more responsible about my post, devoting more time to writing. The more helpful my post is, the more people are willing to share their thoughts. It creates a virtuous circle.

We also have a regular Zoom meeting, and people have volunteered to be the speakers and shared how this reading habit changed their life, how to select good books, how to get most out of the books, etc. And thanks to this book club, I was able to read *[Educated](https://www.amazon.com/Educated-Memoir-Tara-Westover/dp/0399590501)*, *[Tools of Titans](https://www.amazon.com/Tools-Titans-Billionaires-World-Class-Performers/dp/B082VKY29Q/ref=sr_1_1?dchild=1&keywords=tools+of+titans&qid=1619615017&s=books&sr=1-1)*, *[Let My People Go Surfing](https://www.amazon.com/Let-My-People-Go-Surfing-audiobook/dp/B01KB9LY6I/ref=sr_1_1?dchild=1&keywords=Let+My+People+Go+Surfing&qid=1619615047&s=audible&sr=1-1)*, *[Option B](https://www.amazon.com/Option-B-audiobook/dp/B06W9KHNRG/ref=sr_1_1?dchild=1&keywords=Option+B&qid=1619615067&s=audible&sr=1-1)*, *[Hillbilly Elegy](https://www.amazon.com/Hillbilly-Elegy-J-D-Vance-audiobook/dp/B01EM4ZJBO/ref=sr_1_1?dchild=1&keywords=Hillbilly+Elegy&qid=1619615086&s=audible&sr=1-1)*, and *[Post Corona: From Crisis to Opportunity](https://www.amazon.com/Post-Corona-Crisis-Opportunity/dp/B08J9L43P7/ref=sr_1_1?dchild=1&keywords=Post+Corona%3A+From+Crisis+to+Opportunity&qid=1619615103&s=audible&sr=1-1)* (currently reading) just in 5 months, which is the highest record in my reading life.

I am a “people” person. I love meeting people, talking with them, and being inspired by them. But COVID and my newly added role as a mom did not let me do that. The two book clubs that I am in were meant to be an alternative to tackle the situation but they turned out to be an oasis in a desert. It was my first time to have regular online communities like these and I am falling in love with them. I hope I can continue this loose but strong bond with these people even after I go back to work. There is always a way, even better, if you seek for it. This is a key lesson that I have learned during my absence from work.
