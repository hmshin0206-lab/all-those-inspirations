2025 was a year of deliberate transition, both professionally and personally. I moved from leading marketing strategy to taking on business development position at the intersection of blockchain, finance, and product execution. But more than a role change, it was a shift in how I create value. The year reinforced a belief I’ve come to trust: meaningful growth tends to happen where discomfort meets responsibility.

*\* This reflection is guided by the questions proposed in Harvard Business Review’s “[How to Create Your Own Year in Review](https://hbsp.harvard.edu/inspiring-minds/how-to-create-your-own-year-in-review).”*

* * *

## **1\. Reflection: A Year of Strategic Transition**

**Where did I learn the most?**

My steepest learning curve began after accepting an offer to transition from a marketing function into a front-end, deal-focused role. In my previous marketing role, I worked downstream of deal formation. I shaped how already-defined business partnerships and products were positioned, communicated, and delivered to users. That work required deep strategic thinking: understanding user psychology, aligning cross-functional stakeholders, and translating complex technical value propositions into compelling market narratives.

My current role operates one step earlier in the value chain. I now sit at the table during deal formation, partnering directly with external stakeholders and negotiating partnership structures. Instead of amplifying what already exists, I help determine what should exist in the first place.

The ten months following this transition became the steepest learning period of my three years at the company. I absorbed domain-specific knowledge about DeFi liquidity structures, governance mechanisms, and infrastructure integrations at a pace I hadn’t experienced before. The acceleration wasn’t just about technical concepts, but it was about learning to operate with less clarity, fewer precedents, and higher stakes.

I remain genuinely grateful for the opportunity to learn at that depth and speed.

**What am I most proud of?**

I proved to myself and to my organization that the foundation skills I developed in marketing translate powerfully across different functional domains. Structured thinking, cross-stakeholder communication, and disciplined execution aren’t marketing skills. They’re leverage skills that compound when applied to ambiguous, high-stakes problems.

Rather than limiting myself to the boundaries of my previous functional scope, I expanded into owning complex deal structures and product-level understanding. This allowed me to demonstrate that my foundational skills, structured thinking, communication, and execution, translate effectively across different types of roles and responsibilities.

Marketing taught me to think in systems – to understand how user behavior, messaging, product features, and business incentives interconnect. That system-level perspective became an unexpected advantage when navigating the technical and strategic complexity of blockchain partnerships.

**Who enabled me to perform at my best?**

My current team lead played a key role in my transition. She entrusted me with a broad range of responsibilities, which allowed me to study blockchain use cases in a more practical way and gain hands-on exposure to real execution challenges. Through this process, I was able to take on experiences I had not previously encountered and grow quickly into greater ownership.

I’m especially grateful for the trust she placed in me. It created an environment where learning was accelerated, and responsibility felt earned.

## **2\. The Power of Strengths: Building a Reputation for Execution**

**How did my strengths compound this year?**

This year, my strengths compounded not by adding new ones, but by converging across contexts. A career path that spans marketing, product management, and business development became a real advantage when I stepped into a new and highly technical domain. That breadth allowed me to do three things well:

1.  **Adapt quickly** to unfamiliar concepts without getting lost in abstraction
2.  **Ask the right questions early**, because I’ve seen how different functions approach the same problem
3.  **Stay oriented toward execution** rather than endless analysis.

At the core, my strength lies in simplifying complexity and prioritizing action. When faced with unfamiliar concepts or ambiguous problem spaces – whether it’s structuring a DeFi liquidity partnership, negotiating tokenomics alignment, or securing a native USDT integration – I focus on breaking them down into clear decision points and practical next steps.

Over time, this approach translated into trust. Within the organization, I became known as someone who consistently drives work to completion, whose proposals progress with minimal friction because there is accumulated credibility through consistent delivery.

Trust compounds, but only when it’s protected. As my scope expands, I hold myself to a higher standard of precision, accountability, and follow-through. Every proposal carries the weight of past delivery, which means I can’t afford to be careless.

## **3\. Resilience: Professional Growth Beyond Performance**

**What would I approach differently?**

Looking back, I would apply an impact lens earlier when deciding where to focus my energy. In a new team and domain, much of my attention naturally went toward executing assigned tasks well and building a solid foundation. That focus was valuable, but over time I learned that effectiveness compounds when effort is aligned not only with what needs to be done, but with what matters most.

As the year progressed, I became more deliberate about prioritizing work based on potential impact and downstream influence rather than volume or urgency alone. If I were to approach the year again, I would make that shift sooner, using priority and impact as primary guides for energy allocation.

This isn’t about doing less work. It’s about ensuring the work I do creates compounding value rather than linear output.

**Surprises and lessons learned**

One of the most important realizations this year was that growth at senior levels looks fundamentally different than early-career growth. Early on, progress comes from identifying and closing skill gaps—learning what you don’t know and becoming adequate across a broad range of competencies.

As responsibilities expand, however, progress shifts. It’s driven less by closing weaknesses and more by deliberately amplifying strengths to create asymmetric leverage.

-   **Structuring unclear problems** into actionable frameworks
-   **Exercising sound judgement under uncertainty** when data is incomplete
-   **Translate across business and technical domains** to align diverse stakeholders
-   **Consistently turning analysis into execution** rather than letting insights sit in documents

This shift helped me focus not on being broadly adequate at everything, but on being distinctly effective where it matters most. In a world where most people can competently execute, the differentiator is knowing what to execute on and why.

* * *

## **4\. Fast Forward: 2026 and Raising the Output Bar**

**My focus for 2026: Smart leverage through AI**

Looking ahead, my priority is to use AI more intelligently, not as a novelty, but as a genuine force multiplier embedded into how I work. We are entering an era where individuals who know how to collaborate effectively with AI can operate with far greater leverage, not by doing more, but by thinking, deciding, and building faster.

The goal isn’t automation for its own sake. It’s reducing friction across the entire execution cycle so that more energy can be spent on judgment, strategy, and outcome ownership, which are the parts of work that still require distinctly human capabilities.

At the same time, I want to push beyond my current scope by deliberately expanding my technical literacy and hands-on creation capabilities. This means investing in understanding systems not just conceptually, but at an implementation level.

Concretely, I want to:

-   **Build small products** to understand what it feels like to go from idea to working prototype
-   **Experiment with code** to close the gap between strategic understanding and technical execution

Ultimately, 2026 is about widening both scope and coverage. By combining domain expertise, execution judgment, and AI-enabled creation, I want to operate across traditional role boundaries and compound impact in ways that weren’t possible even a few years ago.
