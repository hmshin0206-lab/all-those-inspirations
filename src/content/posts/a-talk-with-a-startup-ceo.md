I try as best as I can to respond to every cold call that I receive from LinkedIn because I understand how desperate they could be when they reach out to me and I believe that this small hospitality can be reciprocal. A few days ago, I got a direct message from the CEO of a tech startup. He said that he just started a new project and is looking for talent for scaling up his business. And he asked me for a cup of coffee to further discuss his ongoing project. I messaged him that I am interested and willing to meet him in person. He seemed a little bit surprised saying that he didn’t expect me to reply. Anyhow, we set a date, and yesterday, I met him at his co-working space in Gangnam and we had a constructive discussion for about two hours. I cannot disclose the full context here due to a confidentiality issue, though I would like to share some of the thoughts that came up to my mind which can rather be applicable in general circumstances.

#### Virtuosos are not natural-born.

Never before developers have been in such high demand than today. Sometimes I also felt envious about the industry trend that values coding skills the most than any other skill. The CEO that I talked with yesterday told me that although his major “happened to be” computer engineering, most of the programming skills that he thinks is fairly top-notch and are proud to share with his developers’ community, as well as Github, were self-taught. He also added that writing codes have many similarities with learning a foreign language; the more you practice, the better you will become. I found this comment especially intriguing since “codes” are normally viewed as a symbol of the science and engineering field, while “language” is that of liberal arts, which suggests that regardless of your role or position in your company, whether you are a PM or a developer, there is no shortcut in learning. It is a process of internalization. Even though there are people who are faster in learning than others, still, **they “earn” it, not get it for free.** So you can be jealous of someone’s hard-working characters or their efforts, but not of what he/she has because you never know how hard they tried to have it.

#### You always need to stay tuned in this fast-changing world.

For the past few years, most of the UI/UX designers have switched their design tool from *Photoshop* to *Sketch*. It is not only because *Sketch* is more user-friendly and intuitive software, but also it was a global trend. I also took several *Sketch* courses in *LinkedIn Learning* since I thought it would be easier for me to communicate well with in-house designers as well as it could add value to my resume. Now, according to him, the trend is changing once again from *Sketch* to *Figma*, an even more powerful tool for UI/UX design and there are a growing number of companies, including his, that are adopting the new software and abandoning the old one. So here is the lesson learned. **If you do not follow up on trends or are being lazy mastering new skills even though the world is urging you to do, it will not take long until you realize that you are outdated.** By the way, I am searching for online courses that I can learn *Figma*.

#### Multiplayers will win eventually.

Although now I can say without hesitation that my career path has been focused on marketing, there was a time that I felt that I was drifting. When I was a junior at LG, I was transferred to a planning and management team and I worked there for about two years, doing finance, project management, and investment. At that time, I felt so much anxious and insecure because I thought I was doing something that is “irrelevant” to marketing. It took me several years until I realized that I was wrong though.

As Steve Jobs mentioned about “connecting the dots” (By the way, this is one of my favorite quotes and I have mentioned it in [my other blog post regarding job interview tips](https://heathershinblog.wordpress.com/2018/10/17/tips-for-those-who-are-hunting-for-jobs-now/) too.), there is no such experience which is completely worthless. In this economic crisis along with the COVID-19, the hurdles for job seekers have even been higher than before and **companies are expecting “multi-players”, which suggests that the spectrum of one’s capacity is required to be wider and deeper.** For instance, companies these days are not just looking for a marketer: they are looking for a person who is capable of marketing, but also has an experience or at least is knowledgeable project management, data analysis, and UX planning.

So now, going back to my case, thanks to my experience in the non-marketing area that I thought was a complete waste of time turned into a great asset in terms of understanding miscellaneous concepts and terminologies that are not commonly used at my work but is generally used in different domains.
