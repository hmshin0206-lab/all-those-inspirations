If someone asks me a keyword of the year 2018, I would say “solidity”. I started my new job after 1.5 years of absence from a professional career, feeling blessed to be able to work again, and especially thankful to keep pursuing my career as a marketer. Although the circumstances that are gaining weight as I grow older sometimes makes it hard for me to try something totally reckless or adventurous that I used to do when I was in my twenties, I think I enjoy these gradual transitions of me becoming more flexible and embracing. And here are three best things that I have done in 2018 as a process of becoming more like “me”.

### 1\. Changing my reading habits

I was never a fast reader. And my reading habit had long been like, “Never start a new book until I finish the one I’m currently reading”. Now that I think about it, this had been a huge obstacle for me to read a great deal in that sometimes it took me so long to finish one book, especially when it was beyond my interests.

![독서 다이어리](/images/posts/2018-reflections-3-best-things-i-ever-did/01-KakaoTalk-Photo-2018-12-30-02-29-23.jpeg)

<small class="caption">‘독서 다이어리 (Reading Diary), the most useful app in 2018. I can easily search books, and quote the parts that I want to remember.</small>

However, early this year, I happened to read a book called ‘Art of Reading’ by Dongjin Lee, a Korean film critic, and a writer. In the book, he wrote that there is absolutely no need to be obsessed with finishing the book you started just because there are so many books in the world that you will never finish reading even if you read incessantly. That is why, according to the book, he spread books everywhere in his house and read it simultaneously. This not only helps you read as many books as possible but also facilitates synergizing the thoughts you gathered from the books.

I followed his method and it worked! I have read over 30 books this year, and am currently reading 6 different books at the same time. (For extensive readers, this might still be a small number but compared to the past, it has been a great improvement for me.) Reading different genres of books at the same time had another merit; it never made me tired of reading. Since I could easily switch from a book to another, I never got bored. For example, I was reading a book about African history, but when the time that I no longer can absorb bits of knowledge comes, I could just easily change it to a novel which required less energy.

### 2\. Rejoining English study club

This year, I rejoined the English club called ‘SG Economist’ that I had participated since 2011. There was almost two years gap while I was in the U.S. doing my masters, but I returned to the club this September and I am happy that I did. The club is basically discussing recent news and social trends in English. Members have come and gone for the last 6 years that I have participated, but half of them are still original crew, meaning that the club is robust and people are so much attached to it. And it has been always an inspiration for me to mingle with people from different backgrounds and share ideas about specific issues.

In addition, since my company has a large presence globally, internal communications are usually in English. Thus, it is crucial for me to maintain the language skills to a certain level. And this club is definitely helping me do that.

### 3\. Taking a drip coffee class

Coffee has always been my favorite. Then, as now, I drink 1~2 cups of coffee every day. One day, I met my former colleague and she told me about the drip coffee class she was taking at that time. I thought it would be great to learn something in depth that you already like. Also, since I enjoyed working at a cafe when I did a book stay this summer (View the post on [Selling An Experience](https://heathershinblog.wordpress.com/2018/08/18/selling-an-experience/)), I thought it would be fascinating to experience the life with coffee and tranquility again. So I enrolled in a coffee class held by a drip cafe called ‘[Lee & Hong](https://www.leeandhong.co.kr/)‘.

What I have learned throughout the 4-week course is that coffee is also food; the fresher coffee beans are, the tastier the coffee gets. However, most of the franchise cafes use the beans that were roasted a few months to even a year ago, focusing too much on the act of drinking coffee, which has been artificially associated with luxurious and fancy images, rather than the taste of it.

The owner of Lee & Hong also told us that the technique of brewing coffee counts at most 10% of the coffee taste and the other 90% is uncontrollable, which is alrady decided throughout the process of harvesting, washing, and roasting etc. Therefore, the subjective elements like what mood you are in now, or whom you are drinking coffee with might matter more.

In conclusion, the coffee class was more like a life lesson to me, allowing me to look something I took for granted entirely differently. The followings are some of the notes I jotted down on Instagram after every class. (in Korean)
