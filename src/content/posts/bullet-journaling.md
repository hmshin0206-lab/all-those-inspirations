This morning, I finally finished reading *[The Bullet Journal Method](https://bulletjournal.com)* by Ryder Carroll. (By the way, I am keeping up well with my Miracle Morning, and reading books has been one of my morning routines. If you want to learn more about my Miracle Morning, please refer to [my previous post](https://heathershinblog.wordpress.com/2020/02/03/i-started-to-wake-up-at-530-in-the-morning/)). My recent interest lies in time management since I am about to face a huge transition in my life personally and this resonates with the very first reason that I picked up this book; to better understand how it works and see if some of the methods mentioned in the book can apply to my own life.

The word ‘bullet journal’ was familiar to me even before reading this book (though I did not grasp the whole concept of it until I read the book) since one of my best friends in a graduate school was doing it. Yet at that time, since her bullet journal was so pretty, I thought it was for practicing one’s sense of design and I never even considered doing it myself since I thought I was far from those people who have a good sense of aesthetics and design. It did not take long for me to realize that I was wrong though; the arts and embellishments when writing a bullet journal are peripheral and **the gist of it is to maximize the flexibility of formats, which I think is the key virtue of bullet journaling.**

![](/images/posts/bullet-journaling/01-img-0414.png)

<small class="caption">There are so many formats of a bullet journal when searching it on Pinterest. But it is not about how “pretty” it looks.</small>

What I liked about this book the most was that it does not just endlessly enumerate complicated methods or techniques about writing a bullet journal, but describes how easy it is to start yet how grand the consequences would be. This book also stresses the importance of having the mindset to keep us going without giving up; for example, the author writes that happiness cannot be a goal itself, but it is rather a result of our action towards the goal. Also, he states that the concept of “perfection” is almost mythological, and we do not have to strive for it. The “perfect” moment, as a relative form, can come anytime anywhere unexpectedly.

![](/images/posts/bullet-journaling/02-img-0188-mosaic.jpg)

<small class="caption">Daily log page in my planner</small>

I found out that the way I have been writing my journal – although it’s neither DIY nor fancy as a bullet journal – is quite similar to some of the main concepts introduced in the book. For instance, I also have my symbols to mark which tasks have been completed, in progress, or procrastinated. Also, I have a list of which I would like to make habits and am keeping track of them every single day. However, I also discovered that I have been focusing too much on managing daily tasks but somehow neglected to ponder on more long-term goals or recording what was happening in the past, which might have been great inspirations or helped me set up other goals to make me move on to the next level. **But now is never late.** I will adopt and reflect on some of the key takeaways from the book in my current journal to make today better than yesterday. And I bet, tomorrow will be even better than today.
