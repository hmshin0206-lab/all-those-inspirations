I think these small touches with human-centered design in mind are what makes a completion of a product. The article reminded me of the Behavioral Analysis class that I took during my graduate studies. Behavioral analysis, also known as behavioral economics is a field of study on how to maximize a reach of a product, service, or promotion to a customer by making a slight twist without his/her recognition.

For example, a research found out that people perceive the volume of juice differently by its container shape. In the experiment, most of the people thought a long and slender glass had a larger quantity compared to a short and wide glass even though both of them were designed with the same capacity. And now if you look around, you come to realize how many drinks or commodity products are produced in long and slender containers.

There is another experiment, conducted to see if a default option influences an increase of organ donations. It was shown that the percentage of people consenting to be donors in the opt-out condition (where respondents were asked whether they would be organ donors and the default was to be the ones) was almost twice as many as that of the people in the opt-in condition (where the default was not to be a donor) and the neutral condition (no default).

These are some of the examples that show certain dispositions of human beings and the analysis behind them. The surprising fact is that these examples are just a tip of the iceberg. There are countless studies that revealed the human psychology and the use of it in a real world.

Some people might criticize it by arguing that it is not right to manipulate people’s mind just because you know more about them. However, I can totally rebut the idea because it can absolutely be used with a good cause or to promote fairness in society. For instance, how you design a questionnaire when you try to do a survey can influence the respondents’ answers. ([Questionnaire Design](http://www.pewresearch.org/methodology/u-s-survey-research/questionnaire-design), Pew Research Center) Thus, I believe that it is important to study behavioral analysis beforehand and to prevent possible distortions from happening.

I would like to conclude this post by introducing a book that is directly related to the subject. The book I recommend is [‘Nudge’](https://www.amazon.com/Nudge-Improving-Decisions-Health-Happiness/dp/014311526X) written by Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein. For me, personally, this book was one of the most inspiring ones of my life (I was so into it that I even mentioned this book in my SOP for graduate school applications). Thus, if you are interested in the concept and want to find out more real-life examples, this book is going to be the right choice to explore and nourish your intellectual curiosity.
