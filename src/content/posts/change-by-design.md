I somehow managed to wake up at 4:30 a.m. this morning and finished the last few chapters of this book. The book is basically a summary of what I have learned through the Design Leadership course at Johns Hopkins with additional real-life cases that the author took on through his career at IDEO.

What I like about design thinking is that it can be applied to anywhere; from something tangible such as designing a product to the intangible things like customer experience. This is possible since the foundation starts from the same concept which is a human-centered mind. (Human in this context means targeted customers)

I also like the idea of it freely switching the ‘creative’ side and the ‘logical’ side throughout the process which have been regarded as the two separate areas. Obviously, some part of design thinking is fast, destructive, and without rules. However, you will need logic to organize and deliver the ideas. The matrix below is a representative example of evaluating innovation suggested in this book, which is definitely on the ‘logical’ side of design thinking.

![](/images/posts/change-by-design/01-5e09f573ea647c6268523a3a50a44f01-1518753118.jpg)

Lastly, I like the pieces of advice that the book is offering in the last chapter which I felt very pragmatic and useful:

-   Fail fast and fail often
-   Listen to the expertise (including the enthusiasts of your product or service)
-   Share your inspirations with your organizations
-   Do not ask ‘what’, ask ‘why’
-   Observe the world with an open mind
-   Sometimes, just watch and do not think (habitual note-taking)
-   Leave as many options as possible
-   Design your life (important!)
