**David Hockney in Seoul (at Seoul Museum of Art)  
Conversation with David Hockney (by Martin Gayford)**

![](/images/posts/conversation-with-david-hockney/01-b612-20190324-142345-732.jpg)

To be honest, I didn’t know him very well until I heard about the exhibition in Korea from one of my colleagues, and he became one of my favorite artists since then. Not only his drawings were magnificent, but also they were well curated, showing the dynamic journeys of his life and how his drawing styles have changed accordingly.

My interest in his drawing after the exhibition led me to read this book that is basically an interview with Hockney (It is no wonder why the title of the book is “Conversation with David Hockney”). His sexual identity, a muse, and inspirations from Pablo Picasso that I had skimmed through the desscription card of each painting were welll-illustrated in the book. Also, the fact that he is an ardent advocate of an iPad inspired me a lot in terms that he keeps trying to learn new things even at his ages (I believe he recently had his 82th birthday).
