I was never a morning person. Even when I was in high school, prepping for the Korean SAT, I rather chose to study until 2 am than waking up at 6. The reason being probably is that I felt my study efficiency peaked at night than in the morning (And I think it did). More importantly, when thinking retrospectively, however, I tried to find inner peace by finishing what should have been done before going to bed than procrastinating it till morning, making a good excuse to wake up late.

The problem is that this habit became so deep-rooted that it continued even after I passed the college entrance exam, graduated, and started working. Even though I did not have any upcoming exams or important presentations, I tended to stay up late, mostly wasting time by doing mobile games, binge-watching YouTube videos or Netflix and so on, and woke up just an hour before leaving for work. Sometimes, my eyes naturally opened up early in the morning like 5 or 6, but I rarely bothered myself to stay up but chose to sleep more even I could only sleep an hour longer.

One day on Jan 13 (I remember the exact date because I wrote it down in my journal), I naturally woke up at 5:40 and decided to stay waking up. Then I wrote a journal about my new year’s resolutions, read a book with my iPad, and opened another blog (in Korean). **It was the most productive two hours in the past 6 months.** From that day, I tried to sleep as early as possible (around 22:30) and set my alarm at 5:30. Sometimes I woke up even before my alarm rang, and sometimes (i.e. weekends) later than that. And here is my morning logs of the past 3 weeks.

<small class="caption">My morning log since Jan 13, 2020</small>

Recently, I joined a month project inside my company, which can be translated as ‘*30-Days-Long Resolution*‘. In Korea, there is a saying that “*You make your resolution and it only lasts for 3 days.*” which implies that setting up a goal is easy but making it last is difficult. This project was initiated to make it last for at least 30 days instead of 3. Now I’m sending a photo of proof with a timestamp in it every morning to the project chat group. I feel like it helps me brace myself better doing it with a group than solo. Until now, I have never missed a day but we will see. I hope I can share later in this blog how things have been changed after 30 days.
