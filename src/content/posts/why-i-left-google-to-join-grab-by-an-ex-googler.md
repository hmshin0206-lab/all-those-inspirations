There are two parts in this post which are ‘why I left Google’, and ‘why I joined Grab’, and I only read the first part. If I remember correctly, it is the first comment that I have ever read or heard that describes the downside of Google. Last year, I had a chance to meet and have a talk with a college alumnus working in Google Korea and asked if there is any drawback of the company or working for the company, and he said, without hesitation, “No”. (And then he added, “Some people might feel pressure because we gotta show our best performance all the time.” and I thought, ‘Wow, is this the only drawback you can think of?’) Therefore, this post that writes Google is no longer innovative was quite a surprise to me.

And then it flipped my thought that maybe, it is a natural sequence for a company to become more conservative and risk-aversive as it grows bigger. This might be comparable to the PLC (Product Life Cycle), one of the frameworks that is often used in business, as it allows huge resources (money and time) put in the ‘introduction’ phase but when the product (or the company in this case) moves to the ‘maturity’ phase, it only needs a minimum input to keep the required output.

![Characteristics-of-the-Product-Life-Cycle-Stages-and-their-Marketing-Implications-1024x613](/images/posts/why-i-left-google-to-join-grab-by-an-ex-googler/01-characteristics-of-the-product-life-cycle-stages-a.png)

In order not to face the ‘decline’ phase which is supposed to come if no further actions are taken, the product (or the company) might need a momentum to remain as the game-changer. And it is true that most of the giant IT companies including Google and Facebook have been seeking for their momentum outside through acquiring prospective tech startups. (i.e. Facebook acquiring Instagram (2012) & Whatsapp (2014), and Google acquiring YouTube (2006) and DeepMind (2014) and so on)

Personally, I think having as many players as possible makes the industry healthier but it seems that this is not the case for the IT industry. I was hoping Snapchat might be the first one which breaks the norm when I heard the news that the company rejected the M&A offer from Facebook but now it seems that Instagram has copied most of the Snapchat’s key features and Snapchat is losing its loyal customers:

[http://mashable.com/2014/01/06/snapchat-facebook-acquisition-2/#p6nJWGQ6PiqM](http://mashable.com/2014/01/06/snapchat-facebook-acquisition-2/#p6nJWGQ6PiqM)

Everything is changing super fast and it is indeed challenging to keep up with the mainstream. I’m thrilled but worried on the other hand as well, how our near future is going to be completely different from what we are seeing now.
