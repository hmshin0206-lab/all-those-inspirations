I had an amazing experience this summer for my holidays. I am not sure if there is a term that defines it but the closest words I can think of are ‘book stay’ and ‘shared economy’, though I still feel that they are not enough to describe the meaning in the full extent.

I stayed at a bookstore & cafe in Jeonju, where a village of Korean traditional houses (called “Hannok”) is located. The owner of the bookstore rents the attic room on the top floor with a very reasonable price ($11/day regardless of peak seasons). Instead, you should work at the cafe at least 4 hours a day. Though, it is not like working as an exchange for the cheap accommodation. Rather, it is more like experiencing as an owner of the bookstore; there is no one who meddles in while you are in the cafe. You can brew coffee for yourself, play the music in your own playlist, and read as many books as you want. Also, since the bookstore is located a few blocks away from its main village area, not many people swing by the place, making it a lot easier for you to work. (definitely not a good news for the “real” owner) During the two days of my shift(?), only three teams visited and had sound and peaceful time in the cafe.

The owner of the bookstore wrote on his blog that he was inspired by the bookshop in Scotland (Related article: [For £36 a night, book-lovers can run a seaside bookshop in Scotland](https://qz.com/1109183/scotlands-airbnb-meets-bookshop-lets-you-play-shop-owner-on-your-next-holiday/)) and thought about implementing the main concept into his own bookstore that was reeling from a deficit. His purpose of running this bookstore instead of just selling it, according to his blog, is not on making money but is on curating great books and providing people with a cozy place to read. (This part explainswhy the accomodation is so cheap) Adding to that, he mentioned that he wanted to support young entrepreneurs with small capital. Currently, two young designers rent this place and covers the rest of the opening hours when there’s no Airbnb guest working at the cafe. After it is closed, they use the space for a calligraphy class.

It might sound odd at first when you think of the concept. Some people asked me with a confused face that why I want to spend my time and money to do “work”. But as the article I shared above mentioned, it makes more sense when you put it as people are not paying for their labor but are paying for the pleasure (=experience).

Of course, the accommodation was not comparable to a 5-star hotel and there were definitely rooms for improvement. Yet, this made me think of the possibility of this whole new business concept that we are not familiar yet and so cannot be defined with existing terms. Also, I valued the owner’s mindset, pursuing his own belief while keeping up only the minimum requirements to run the business. Hopefully, there are more places like this one in the near future that sells not high-quality products or fancy services, but precious experience that someone might have been eager to do once in their lifetime.
