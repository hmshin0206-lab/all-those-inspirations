Living in this era with a lot, and sometimes overwhelming amount of information, life hacks, and advice from all around the world, one keyword that has been bothering me for the last couple of years was “efficiency”; how to be good at not just absorbing knowledge but at retaining it so that I can keep a stash of relevant expertise in my mind and use them whenever necessary.

One way to do that is to jot down everything that impresses you or that you have to remember and keep those notes with you. For example, you can write down a nice quote from a book that you are reading, or the main ideas from a YouTube video that you just watched. You can even archive posts on Instagram while scrolling down your feed.

Following my own advice, I tried archiving everything noteworthy I came across. However, I soon realized that I had countless pages of random content on my *Evernote* and *Notion* accounts as well as on my physical notes but I never looked at them. I was so carried away gathering new nuggets of knowledge that I did not put enough effort to digest, organize, and develop my own thoughts on the things I was collecting.

Luckily, I ended up coming across this YouTube video (in Korean) and it was the perfect answer to my struggles. The speaker who introduces himself as a professor and also an archivist explains the best way to take notes that sticks.

Here are his steps:

1.  **Pause and think about the main ideas (= “momentary thoughts”.)**
2.  **Connect the ideas (= “linking the thoughts”)**
3.  **Do not excerpt; write it using your own words.**
4.  **Categorize your ideas and organize them.**

After watching the video, I realized that he was not explaining anything new. There is already an existing term for this specific note-taking method that is formally known as “Zettelkasten.” Zettelkasten, also called the “slip-box” method, was first introduced by S. Ahrens in his book ‘How to Take Smart Notes’.

<iframe loading="lazy" class="youtube-player" width="640" height="360" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/BG1tfC7tSYw?version=3&amp;rel=1&amp;showsearch=0&amp;showinfo=1&amp;iv_load_policy=1&amp;fs=1&amp;hl=en&amp;autohide=2&amp;wmode=transparent" allowfullscreen="true" style="border:0;" sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin allow-popups allow-presentation allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox"></iframe>

The steps introduced in the book are slightly different from those introduced in the previous video, with the additional concept of fleeting notes, literature notes, and permanent notes. Even so, the main idea stays the same; **to involve an active thought process while you are reading or watching something new**. This is especially critical because if you write and think simultaneously, you do not just write things down passively but you are actively training your brain to understand and retain information.

As a starter, I decided to look back my existing pages and organize them this morning. Only then could I readily use all the knowledge I had gathered whenever the need presented itself. I also took out my fountain pen I hadn’t used in eternity and leaked some ink out of it. I hope this gesture marks the start of a new journey in my life to take better notes. By taking smarter notes and archiving them better, I would not only be expanding my brain but also gain in efficiency.
