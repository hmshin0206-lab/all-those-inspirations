## “So, How Effective Was Your Marketing Campaign?”

The thing I enjoyed the most about my new job when I first joined the company was the fact that I could measure the performances of the marketing campaigns that I designed. Obviously, I’m working in IT so everything can be interpreted with “numbers”, but since my previous company put more emphasis on offline marketing such as trade show and in-store management – not only because it was selling consumer electronics (tangible products), but also the concept of “digital marketing” was still new up until just a few years ago – I, as a marketer, was challenged by those who either downplays marketing or those who do not grasp the ideas of branding and marketing. And they used to ask “So, how can you prove that your marketing campaigns have contributed to the sales increase?” Unfortunately, I had no answer for that. However, in my current company in which the main product is an app (service), it became much easier for me to communicate with my stakeholders when I try to convey the importance of marketing.

Today, I would like to share some of the key indexes used in digital marketing (or at least some of the indexes mainly used in my company). In this post, I mention only quantitative indexes although we normally check both quantitative and qualitative (like users’ reactions) data when analyzing the result.

## 1\. Service Data

Service data (I am not sure if other companies call it the same) is fundamental data that supports the service itself. Therefore, if the service data is on the wane every year, it means that it is in on downturn, thus extra care is needed to revive it or the service will die eventually. For example, in the service that I’m currently in charge of, the followings are the service data to be monitored every day: 

-   Page View
-   Post
    -   Genuine Post: Number of posts made by users (Since in the service that I’m on now, businesses can also write posts for advertisement purposes, it is important to check how many posts are created by users organically)
-   Post View
-   Duration Time
-   Likes
-   Comments

## 2\. Event Participation Data

### KPI

KPIs must be set differently based on the characteristics of events. It can be the number of posts or likes. However, the retention rate is much more important than the increase of posts or likes during the event, since the primary purpose of such events is to raise the service data. Hence, our team checks MoM and WoW as well in order to see the retention rate. (But I admit it is very difficult to make the users gained through the campaigns stick to the service after the event.)

### PR Data

-   CTR = Clicks / Impressions  
    This is one of the most commonly used indicators to measure the effectiveness of a banner (or any types of materials that are shown to users). The threshold to discern whether the banner was effective or not might differ by industry and product, but we typically say that it effective when the CTR is over 0.01 (=1%), meaning 1 person out of 100 views attempts to click the banner.

2018 “May-sick” event planned right after Japanese golden week to diagnose what stage of your laziness had a great hit, reaching 11.6% of banner CTR on the first day of the event.

### User Journey

-   Bounce Rate (opposite to Retention Rate)  
    If the event has been designed with more than one step, it is pivotal to check how many users have left the page before completing the flow as planned. This will help you to design better and more efficient user flow the next time. For instance, our company recently launched an internal system to ease making a certain type of events. And throughout the initial rounds of events, we found out that over 20% of users who happened to visit the Intro Page, in which has a ‘start’ button, left the page immediately. But we also found out that the bounce rate dropped dramatically (to less than 5%) once the users clicked on the ‘start’ button and went for the next step, meaning that the content of the event was not a problem in this case. Therefore, we decided to adjust the system by operationalizing the Intro Page in order to minimize the bounce rate. This is a great example of “numbers” providing you with insights to improve the current status.

We found out that the bounce rate of the Intro Page to the next one was over 20%, so we decided to get rid of the Intro Page by adjusting the system.
