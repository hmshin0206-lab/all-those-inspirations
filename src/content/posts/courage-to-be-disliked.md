I just finished reading the last chapter of this book after flipping the first page two days ago. Considering that I normally take a month to read a book (just because skimming through is not my expertise), this book is easy to read and has a gripping story. Unlike the expectation implied from the title *Courage to be Disliked*, the scope of the book is not limited to how to handle others’ criticism but presents a life lesson from a holistic view in alignment with an idea created by a psychologist named Alfred Adler. His idea seems quite unique and against our common sense yet gradually I was convinced to the theory.

First, it refuses ‘causationism’ and supports ‘teleology’ by saying that every action of a human being is not caused by each individual’s past experience but is to fulfill a certain purpose that you set either consciously or unconsciously. Therefore, finding a fault from the past – trauma, so to speak – is merely an excuse to make you feel better or comfortable. And with this, the foundation of this philosophy starts from the notion that everyone can change no matter what they have gone through in the past.

Second, Adler also claims that a life is not a form of a ‘line’ that has ups and downs until the time of death but a ‘succession of moments’ which basically means every one of them matters so much that we have to focus on living in the present, not in the past or the future. In order to live in the present, sometimes we need the courage to be disliked, and the courage to be ordinary.

I admit that some part of the message he tries to convey may sound too ideal to some of you, but it lightened me up in terms of encouraging us to live a life of our own, not others. Also, we do not have to judge the quality of our life based on others’ standards, which means I am the only one who has every right to decide how happy I am now. In that sense, I felt that this book is providing readers a healing energy that makes me embrace and appreciate this right moment.
