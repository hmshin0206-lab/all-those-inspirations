A few weeks ago, I received an invitation from Google for ‘Google in Korea’ event. Ever since I visited Google Korea office for the first time in 2017, with an invitation from one of the alumni in my university, I was always had a good impression about the company and how it respects its employees to its full extent.

![](/images/posts/i-went-to-google-in-korea-event/01-mmstdqwz.jpeg)

<small class="caption">I visited Google Korea office in Aug 2017 for the first time.</small>

It turned out that this event was the first official one held by Google Korea. The purpose was to deliver what Google’s mission especially in Korean market is and how it is doing to tackle the challenges the company is facing with these agendas. This event was only for those in the Sales & Marketing Division and the Google Cloud. After listening to a keynote speech from John Lee, the CEO of Google Korea, the session was split into two for panel discussions. I was in the Sales & Marketing session.

![](/images/posts/i-went-to-google-in-korea-event/02-screen-shot-2019-11-09-at-1-46-23-pm.png)

<small class="caption">An invitation letter from Google Korea</small>

There are some thoughts that I had during the talk that I would like to share in this blog. I am basically copying what I jot down in my note simply because I want to remember them as raw and vivid as they are now.

-   All the panels (their titles were from team leaders to country director) were **excellent speakers**. They understood the essence of every question and stated their opinions very clearly without any pause, which I admired so much since I think I’m not yet good at impromptu speech, of which the essence is to organize and deliver my thoughts on the spot.
-   The panels were from different backgrounds but they have been through so-called an “elite course” in their careers. In spite of this, however, they were very respectful and lowering themselves. One of the panels said **“humble” might be the keyword to represent Google** and I could see that through watching them. They also shared that **Google does prefer people who are “passionate” but is not very fond of the ones who are too “competitive”**.
-   I was not surprised but witnessed that the overall company culture is somewhat similar to that of LINE; it is very open without any hierarchies, and the company tries its best efforts to suit what its employees need or might need. It’s like; “The company will do everything for you to make you feel comfortable working in Google. **You just need to prove that you are worth it!**“.

After the panel session, we went up to a canteen and had a networking session. I had some conversations with some of the panels (including Taewon Kim who has published some books including “[Letters from a Young Googler: Relive Your Dead Passion](https://www.google.com/search?newwindow=1&rlz=1C5CHFA_enUS738US738&sxsrf=ACYBGNSlTp2c4SdxDQssaM3s_pyW2KerFw%3A1573285868463&ei=7G_GXav1G5K-wAPBkbb4Dw&q=%EC%A0%8A%EC%9D%80+%EA%B5%AC%EA%B8%80%EB%9F%AC%EC%9D%98+%ED%8E%B8%EC%A7%80&oq=%EC%A0%8A%EC%9D%80+%EA%B5%AC%EA%B8%80%EB%9F%AC&gs_l=psy-ab.3.0.0.49784.57110..58018...20.4..4.132.2955.0j26......0....1..gws-wiz.....10..0i71j0i131j0i3j35i362i39j0i30j0i5i30j0i8i30j0i10i30j33i160.F2qJsQx6gj0)” which I read when I was a college student and is still one of my favorite books.)

Overall, it was such a meaningful experience as well as a great motivation to me in terms that I had a deeper understanding about the company and how Googlers work. Also, I began to think about what my next step should be as a digital marketer in an IT industry and as a future leader in an organization.
