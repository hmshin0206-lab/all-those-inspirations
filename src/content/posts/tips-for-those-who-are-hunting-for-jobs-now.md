This time of the year is one of the most hectic seasons for college seniors who are hunting for jobs since a lot of job interviews are scheduled during October and November.

A few weeks ago, I had a chance to participate in a job interview as an interviewer. Since I was always in the position of an “interviewee” before that, this whole interview process with a different perspective reminded me of the past interviews I went through and helped me contemplate what I should have and should not have done. So here are some of my personal tips for those who are currently preparing for a job interview (or any other interviews).

### Prepare a one-minute speech that is intriguing enough to lead questions

It is a 99% chance that interviewers will start the session by asking you to introduce yourself briefly. The reason behind this stale and boring question is not only because they are curious about you but also because self-introduction is a good place to start the conversation and interviewers can come up with leading questions based on your story. Some interviewers are too busy to read up your application essays. (※ As for many other countries including the U.S., applicants usually need to submit only a resume, but in South Korea, applicants are required to submit essays along with the resume. The essay questions are different by companies.) Therefore, it is better to prepare a one-minute speech assuming that they know nothing about you and emphasize a few but unique characteristics or experiences of yours. When I was a college student, I prepared my one-minute introduction on how I could earn money instead of spending it during my 4 years in university. Then it led to a story about how many times I received scholarships, and how I won prizes in two contests on business strategies. The cool thing about having an impact in your first introduction speech is that in that way, you can expect possible leading questions and be prepared for them as well. Also, you can have a better chance to leave a good impression among hundreds or thousands of applicants.

### Don’t mention the skills that you are not confident

I saw so many people fill up their LinkedIn profile and resume with the skills that they are not “skillful” at all. It’s like, you have learned Japanese in high school as a mandatory course and never used it after the graduation, ending up being able to recall very few words, but you still add Japanese in your ‘skill’ section. This might raise your chance of being noticed and getting invited to the interview, but if interviewers find out that you are not actually good at it, it worsens your evaluations even more as they have already expected so much from your resume.

At the interview that I mentioned above (the one that I participated as an interviewer), the applicant was a Taiwanese. Since at that time, we were recruiting a marketer for a Taiwanese market, we thought that she might be the good fit. And she emphasized in her resume that she is very fluent in Korean. She even attached some of her Korean language certificates as supplements. However, it did not take long till we figured out she had a hard time understanding even a very basic question in Korean. Although Korean language skill was not even the mandatory requirement, we were disappointed because we expected it from her resume. It would have been a better scenario if she did not mention her Korean skills in her resume and show (like “Ta-da!”) that she could speak the language during the interview. Making a long story short, if you think that your skill is not enough (“enough” in this context means enough to be used in a workplace), it is better not mentioning it at all.

### You’ve gotta have a story that makes your career look consistent

I admit that it is very rare to find your so-called “calling” in your first job. The reason is very simple; basically, you do not have enough experience to discern which one suits you better and which one does not especially if you just graduated a college. However, not many people have the courage to change their current status so they end up sticking to the job they loathe and complaining about it every day. If you decided to take a chance and give it a try though, (first of all, wish you all the best!) you have to have (or at least make up) a concrete reason that led you to decide to change your career.

You might need some brainstorming to make your story more convincing. The easiest way to do that, from my own experience, is “connecting the dots” as [Steve Jobs had mentioned in his speech at the Stanford](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D1R-jKKp3NA), which is something you think is totally irrelevant to the other might have a little bit of connection. And you can start from there. Even though I’m in the marketing field now, I also had one and a half year of my time working in finance & investment field, longing for marketing and feeling quite miserable every single day. Looking back, though, I thought this experience could be my competitiveness since not many marketers have experiences in these kinds of number-crunching jobs. For that reason, I created my own story which emphasized the importance of measuring marketing performances as well as being able to think out-of-the-box. For that reason, my LinkedIn profile headline had long been “A marketing strategist with half science and half art in mind”. And I think this worked out well for many interviews I have gone through.

### Don’t blame yourself

Last but not least, even if it did not work out, please keep in mind that it is never your fault. I have witnessed many young students who blamed themselves for not getting into the position they aspired. This kind of thought is never helpful and is self-destructive. Sometimes the hiring process is not as simple and straightforward as getting into a college, which means it is not like you will have a higher chance to be accepted if you have a higher SAT score. In most cases for job recruiting, a “fit” is a significant factor that might turn the result completely different. (And as you know, a “fit” is a very subject term.) Therefore, even though you did not get the chance this time, it is not because of your lack of competence. If there should be one to be blamed, it is the economic condition that can never reach the unemployment rate to 0%. So, there is absolutely no need to feel like the end of the world.
